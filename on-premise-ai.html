The problem with cloud AI

Every API Call Sends Your Data
Somewhere Else.

When you use a cloud AI API — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Azure — your data travels to a third-party data centre for processing. For most businesses, that is an acceptable tradeoff. For yours, it may not be.

Client documents, patient records, financial data, legal correspondence, proprietary research — the moment these pass through an external API, your data sovereignty claim ends. Your privacy policy becomes contingent on a third party's. Your compliance posture depends on their terms of service, which can change.

On-premise AI eliminates this entirely. The model runs on your hardware. The data never leaves your network. Inference happens inside your perimeter — not theirs.

The performance gap between local and cloud models has effectively closed for most enterprise use cases. Mistral, Llama 3, and Qwen at 7B–70B now match GPT-3.5 and approach GPT-4 on most practical tasks. The capability is available. The question is where it runs.

Cloud AI — data risk
Your data transits and is processed on third-party infrastructure. Subject to their data retention policies, government data requests, and terms-of-service changes. Not suitable for regulated data categories.
Private cloud / VPC — partial control
Data stays in your cloud tenant, but you remain dependent on the cloud provider's infrastructure, pricing, and availability. Data sovereignty is contractual — not technical.
On-premise — full sovereignty
Model, data, and inference run on hardware you own and control. Zero third-party dependency at any point in the pipeline. The only architecture that provides genuine, technical — not contractual — data sovereignty.

Infrastructure

What Hardware
You Actually Need.

We assess your existing infrastructure before recommending anything. Many clients already have sufficient hardware. New investment is less common than assumed.

Tier 1 — Entry
Single mid-range GPU

6–8GB VRAM (RTX 3090, RTX 4080, A10G). Sufficient for 7B parameter models at 4-bit quantization. Handles moderate concurrent users and document-scale workloads.

Runs: Mistral 7B · Llama 3 8B · Phi-3 · Gemma 2 9B
Tier 2 — Production
Dual GPU or A100-class

24–48GB VRAM (2× RTX 4090, A100 40GB, L40S). Supports 13B–34B models at full precision, or 70B at quantization. Enterprise throughput, low latency, multi-user concurrency.

Runs: Llama 3 70B (Q4) · Qwen 72B (Q4) · Mistral 8×7B
Tier 3 — High-throughput
Multi-GPU server or CPU cluster

80GB+ VRAM (A100 80GB, H100) or high-RAM CPU server (256GB+ RAM). Full-precision 70B models, high-concurrency production workloads, or air-gapped environments without GPU access.

Runs: Any model at full precision · Custom fine-tuned models

Hardware assessment is part of every engagement. We scope to your existing infrastructure first — new hardware procurement is only recommended when the use case genuinely requires it.

FAQ

On-Premise AI
Questions.

What does on-premise AI deployment mean?
On-premise AI deployment means the entire AI system — the model, the inference engine, the data pipeline, and the application layer — runs on servers that you own and control. No data is sent to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or any other third-party API. Every inference happens inside your network perimeter. This is the only architecture that provides genuine data sovereignty.
Which industries require on-premise AI deployment?
Industries with regulatory, contractual, or reputational requirements for data sovereignty include: legal services (client confidentiality), healthcare and medical (patient data, HIPAA), financial services and banking (PCI DSS, MiFID II), government and public sector, defence and critical infrastructure, and any business processing sensitive personal data under GDPR. Increasingly, enterprise businesses with proprietary data also require on-premise deployment to protect competitive advantage.
What local LLM models do you deploy?
We deploy quantized open-weight models suited to the client's hardware and use case. Commonly deployed models include Mistral 7B and 8×7B, Llama 3 (8B and 70B), Phi-3, Gemma 2, and Qwen 2.5. Model selection is driven by the performance-hardware tradeoff analysis we conduct during the audit phase. We do not recommend a model before understanding your infrastructure and workload requirements.
What hardware do I need for on-premise AI?
Requirements depend on the model size and workload. A 7B parameter model running at 4-bit quantization requires approximately 6–8GB VRAM — a single mid-range GPU is sufficient. A 70B model requires 40–48GB VRAM. For CPU-only inference, 32–64GB RAM supports 7B models at lower throughput. We assess your existing infrastructure first — many clients already have hardware sufficient for production-grade on-premise AI without new capital expenditure.
How long does an on-premise AI deployment take?
A standard on-premise RAG pipeline or document intelligence system deploys in 4–6 weeks. More complex agentic systems take 6–10 weeks. On-premise deployments add approximately 1–2 weeks to equivalent cloud timelines, primarily for infrastructure assessment, model quantization, and containerisation. All timelines are agreed in writing before work begins.
Do we retain access to your system after deployment?
No. After handoff, we retain zero access to your infrastructure, your model, or your data. All source code, configuration files, and credentials are transferred to you in full. Our involvement ends at your instruction. This is not a managed service by default — though we offer optional support retainers for clients who prefer it.
